Privacy Policy

Oddly Us · Effective 15 September 2026

Oddly Us is a question-card app for two people sharing one phone. It has no accounts, no sign-up and no partner invitations. It never records, stores or transmits anything either of you says out loud.

The short version

Your answers are never collected. The app has no text field, no microphone access and no recording of any kind. What you say to each other stays between you.

Your reading progress stays on your phone. Which pack you are playing and how far through it you are never leave the device.

We count a handful of anonymous events — such as "a card was viewed" or "a purchase completed" — so we can tell whether the app is working. These are not tied to your name, your email or your device's advertising identifier.

We do not track you across apps or websites, show ads, or sell anything about you to anyone.

Who is responsible

Oddly Us is the publisher of this app and is responsible for the data described here. For anything on this page, including a request about your data, write to oddlyusapp@gmail.com. We aim to reply within 30 days.

What stays on your phone

The following is written to your device's local storage and is never sent to us or to anyone else. It is not backed up to a server, does not sync between devices, and is not restored when you restore a purchase.

  • Which deck you are currently playing.
  • For each deck you have started: the order its cards are in, where you are in it, how many distinct cards you have seen, and whether you have finished it.
  • Whether you have finished or skipped the three-card how-to-play guide, and which step you last saw.
  • A note of whether the App Store has confirmed your purchase, so the packs you own still open when you are offline.
  • A note of which one-time events have already been counted, so opening the app twice is not counted as two new users.

Deleting the app removes all of it. We cannot recover it, and neither can you — which is why reinstalling starts your decks fresh even though your purchase can be restored.

What the app sends, and to whom

Usage analytics — PostHog

We use PostHog to count a small, fixed set of events. This is how we tell whether people who open the app actually start playing, and whether the purchase screen works. The complete list of events the app can send is:

EventWhat travels with it
App opened for the first timeNothing beyond the common properties below.
First card viewedThe deck's ID and the card's ID.
First question advancedThe deck's ID.
A deck was openedThe deck's ID.
The Starter Pack was completedNothing further.
Purchase screen viewed, purchase started, purchase completedWhich screen the offer was opened from.
Restore purchases finishedThe outcome, and whether it was run from Settings or the offer.
The how-to-play guide was opened, stepped through, or leftWhich of the three steps, and whether it was a first launch or a replay from Settings.

Card IDs are identifiers such as starter_004. The text of a question is never sent, and nothing records whether a question was answered, skipped, liked or disliked — the app has no way of knowing.

PostHog also attaches, automatically:

  • A random identifier generated on your device the first time the app runs. It is not derived from any hardware identifier, is not your Apple ID, and is not shared with any other app. It is erased when you delete the app.
  • A session identifier that is regenerated every time the app launches.
  • The app's version and build number, its bundle identifier, your iOS version and a broad device type such as "Mobile".
  • The time the event happened.

PostHog's servers see the IP address your request arrives from, as any web server does. We have switched off PostHog's location lookup, so that address is not turned into a country, region or city on our events. We have also switched off session replay (no screen recording), error and crash capture, in-app surveys, and PostHog's user-profile feature, so no profile of you is built up over time. The app never calls PostHog's identify function, because there is nobody to identify.

Event data is sent to PostHog Cloud EU and is stored in the European Union.

Purchases — RevenueCat and Apple

The one-time purchase that unlocks the five paid packs is handled by Apple. We never see, receive or store your card details, your billing address or your Apple ID.

We use RevenueCat to check with Apple whether a purchase is valid and still active — that check is the only thing that unlocks paid content. When you buy or restore, RevenueCat receives an anonymous app user ID it generates on your device, Apple's receipt for the transaction, your App Store country, and basic device and app version information. It does not receive your name or email address, and we do not ask it to collect your device's advertising identifier.

Apple's own handling of your purchase is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Support email

If you write to us, we receive whatever you put in the message, and we keep it only as long as it takes to answer you. Please do not include anything sensitive; a support email is not a secure channel.

What we never collect

  • Anything either of you says. The app does not request microphone access and has no field for typing an answer.
  • Your name, email address, phone number or date of birth. There is no account to create.
  • Your location. The app does not request location access, and we have disabled the IP-based lookup that would otherwise infer a country.
  • Your advertising identifier (IDFA). The app contains no advertising SDK and does not ask for permission to track you. You should never see App Tracking Transparency's "Allow app to track" prompt from Oddly Us. If you ever do, it is a bug — please tell us.
  • Your contacts, photos, calendar, health data or anything about your partner.
  • Which questions you answered or how you answered them.

Why we are allowed to do this

If you are in the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area, the UK GDPR and the GDPR require us to name a lawful basis for each use:

  • Purchases and restoring access — necessary to perform our contract with you. Without checking the entitlement we cannot give you what you paid for.
  • The small set of usage events — our legitimate interest in knowing whether a new app functions and is worth continuing. We have kept this to a fixed list of events with no profile, no location and no cross-app tracking specifically to keep that interest from outweighing your privacy.

How long anything is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete the app.
  • Analytics events: retained by PostHog under our project's retention settings and deleted thereafter.
  • Purchase records: kept by RevenueCat and by Apple for as long as needed to support your purchase, handle refunds, and meet tax and accounting obligations.
  • Support email: deleted once the conversation is finished.

Your rights, and one honest limitation

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to our use of it, or to complain to your data protection authority. In the UK that is the ICO; in the EEA it is your national authority.

There is a limitation you should know about, and it is a consequence of how little we collect. We have no way of connecting an analytics record to you as a person. There is no account, no email address and no device identifier on our side that we could match against a request from you. If you ask us to find or delete "your" data, we genuinely cannot locate it from your name or email alone, and the law does not require us to start collecting more information about you in order to be able to. We will not pretend otherwise.

What you can do instead:

  • Delete everything held on your device by deleting the app. That also ends any further event collection, permanently, because the random identifier goes with it.
  • Ask us about your purchase. Purchase records can be found from your App Store receipt, so write to us and we will help.
  • Ask us anything about this policy at oddlyusapp@gmail.com, and we will answer.

Selling and sharing

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are used in California's CCPA/CPRA. We have never done so. We share data only with the two service providers named above, each of which processes it on our instructions and for no purpose of their own.

International transfers

Analytics data is stored in the European Union. RevenueCat and Apple operate internationally and may process purchase data outside your country, under the safeguards described in their own privacy policies.

Children

Oddly Us is written for adult couples and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 13, or under 16 in regions where that is the threshold. If you believe a child has used the app and you would like us to look into it, write to us.

Security

Data in transit to PostHog, RevenueCat and Apple is encrypted with HTTPS. Data on your phone is protected by iOS's own app sandbox and device encryption. No system is perfect, but the best protection here is structural: we hold very little, and none of it identifies you.

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app collects, we will update this page and change the effective date at the top. Material changes will be noted in the app's release notes. The version you agreed to is the one in force when you used the app; this page always shows the current one.
